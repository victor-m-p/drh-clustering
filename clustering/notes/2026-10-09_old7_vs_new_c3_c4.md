# Old 7-cluster solution vs. new c = 3 and c = 4 (2026-10-09)

Trail note before switching to the new DRH dump. Same data as the manuscript (SCCSR.v1, June 2024): 358 group entries, 36 questions. **Everything that differs from the manuscript is due to method changes, not data.**

## What changed in the clustering

1. **Log-likelihood for BIC/AIC.** The old code scored each row by its fit to the cluster it was assigned to (`sum q * log P(row | cluster)`). That leaves out the cost of not knowing the cluster (about log c per row), so extra clusters looked almost free. Now: the mixture log-likelihood `sum_i w_i log sum_r pi_r P(row_i | r)`, with n = 358 entries (was 376 expanded rows).
2. **Mixing proportions (pi).** Cluster sizes are now estimated (previously all equal, 1/c).
3. **Weights.** The saved solution was previously refit without the expansion weights (and from a new random start). Now the best fit from the restarts is saved directly, with weights.
4. **Restarts.** 100 random starts per c, keeping the best by log-likelihood (was one start per c).

The corrected BIC is lowest at **c = 3 (8616.9)**, with **c = 4 close behind (8628.1)**. The old code picked 7.

## What holds up, and what doesn't

**Holds up:**
- **The two ancient packages are distinct.**
  - Old 5 maps entirely onto the new minimal ritual package (43 of 43): ritual monitoring without moral monitoring, no conversion, worship of other gods allowed, a hungry and anthropomorphic god.
  - Old 3 maps mostly onto the royal/hungry package (46 of 53 at c = 3).
  - Of the 39 entries starting before 500 BCE, 33 are in these two packages at c = 3 (17 minimal, 16 royal/hungry). The manuscript's "most cultures before ~500 BCE are dims 3 or 5" still holds, in coarser form.
- **The main contrast.** Moralizing, proselytizing, exclusive packages with an unquestionably good god that has no hunger, versus a minimal, permissive package with a hungry, anthropomorphic god. This is the c = 3 split in its clearest form.
- **Islamic dynasties vs. reform movements.** Old 2 (dynasties) goes to the royal/hungry package. Old 4 (reform) goes to broad moralizing, and at c = 4 splits between abstract god (27) and maximal moralizing (28).
- **Old 4's "less anthropomorphic, lower emotion" profile.** At c = 4 it reappears in stronger form as the abstract god package. That package now crosses traditions, also drawing in Quakers, Jain, Pure Land and Zoroastrian entries.

**Does not hold up (at c = 3 or 4):**
- **A modern package with little ritual monitoring (old 0).** Every new cluster has ritual observance of 0.86–1.00. Old 0 is absorbed into broad moralizing (57 of 57); at c = 4 it splits into maximal (45) and abstract (12). **The discussion paragraph on rituals and CREDs relies on this finding.** It may or may not come back with more data; at c ≥ 5 the solutions are currently too unstable to tell.
- **Old 0 vs. old 1** (Protestant vs. early Christian/Catholic, differing on anthropomorphism and sky deity). Both merge into maximal moralizing.
- **Old 6** was never interpreted. It splits across all new packages and doesn't correspond to anything coherent.
- **The temporal-dynamics claims in Fig 2B** ("bursty" rise of dim 1 around year 0 and of dim 2 around 500 CE; gradual rise of dims 0 and 4). Not re-checked yet; this needs the over-time figure redone for the new solutions.

## Open questions before or with the new data

- **c = 3 or 4?** BIC slightly prefers 3; the abstract god split at 4 looks substantively interesting. Probably report both, and check whether the new data separates them more clearly.
- **35 entries without a supreme high god** are clustered with all 16 high-god questions treated as missing. Decide whether to exclude them, or treat "no supreme high god" as information.
- **Whether the ritual-light modern package reappears** with more data. If not, drop it from the discussion.

## How these numbers were made

- **Old 7:** `data/EM/EM_{theta,q}_group.csv` at commit `21c384c` (the manuscript run). Entry-level q is the mean over expanded rows.
- **New:** `clustering/data/EM_{theta,q}_group_c{3,4}.csv` from `clustering/run_clustering.py`. Entry-level q is the weighted sum over expanded rows.
- Each entry is assigned to its highest-q cluster for the cross-tabulations and the median years.
- Christian, Islamic and Buddhist shares come from `modeling/entry_tags.csv`.
- **Figures:** `clustering/fig/dimensions/EM_{dimensions,profile}_group_c{3,4}.jpg`. The published 7-cluster heatmap is `figures/EM_dimensions.jpg`.
